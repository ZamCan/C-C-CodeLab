DECISION TABLES — THREE CONDITIONS, EIGHT RULES

A decision table systematically maps combinations of conditions to actions. For three independent yes/no conditions, there are 2³ = 8 possible combinations.

Hypothetical example, not an official admission rule:
S = relevant subject requirement met
G = required grade threshold met
A = application complete
Action E = eligible for review if and only if S AND G AND A.

RULE | S | G | A | ACTION
R1   | Y | Y | Y | E
R2   | Y | Y | N | Not met
R3   | Y | N | Y | Not met
R4   | Y | N | N | Not met
R5   | N | Y | Y | Not met
R6   | N | Y | N | Not met
R7   | N | N | Y | Not met
R8   | N | N | N | Not met

Read R3: the subject requirement is met and the application is complete, but grades are insufficient; therefore the hypothetical combined rule fails.

Decision table components:
- Condition stub: the questions or conditions being checked.
- Condition entries: Y/N or other relevant values for each rule.
- Action stub: the possible actions.
- Action entries: which action is selected under each rule.

Completeness: include every relevant combination, or justify a 'don't care' symbol when a condition genuinely cannot affect an action. Consistency: avoid contradictory actions for the same rule.

Exercise: Modify the hypothetical policy so that an incomplete application is 'Pending documents', regardless of S or G. How many rules change? Explain which conditions become irrelevant when A = N.
